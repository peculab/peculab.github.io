    I’m pleased to share that our paper, “FedQPA: Quantum-Inspired Hypernetwork Personalization for Communication-Constrained Federated Wearable IoT Networks,” has been published in the proceedings of IEEE ICCCN 2026 and is now available on IEEE Xplore. https://lnkd.in/gRjxjhmt Wearable stress detection is inherently personal. Physiological patterns vary significantly across individuals, yet federated learning systems must also operate within the limited communication and computing capacity of wearable devices. This creates an important question: How can we personalize models for heterogeneous users without repeatedly transmitting the entire model? To address this challenge, we propose FedQPA, a quantum-inspired hypernetwork personalization framework for federated wearable learning. FedQPA freezes the LSTM backbone and trains only a lightweight shared generator. Using a rotation-inspired trigonometric mapping, the generator dynamically produces LoRA-style low-rank adaptation weights for the classification head. The model therefore remains fully classical while incorporating a useful quantum-inspired inductive bias. Our experimental results demonstrate a promising communication–robustness trade-off: 🔹 FedQPA: Macro MCC 0.205 at approximately 20 KB per round 🔹 FedAvg: Macro MCC 0.136 at approximately 500 KB per round 🔹 FedLoRA: Macro MCC 0.095 at approximately 4 KB per round 🔹 Hybrid QLSTM: Macro MCC 0.103 at approximately 140 KB per round Across ten random seeds, FedQPA also achieved a mean best-over-rounds Macro MCC of 0.282 ± 0.082, suggesting that its performance was not limited to a single favorable initialization. The broader lesson from this study is that effective federated personalization may depend less on transmitting a larger model and more on designing a compact and expressive adaptation mechanism. Instead of federating the entire recurrent network, or relying on a fixed low-rank update, FedQPA federates a lightweight generator capable of producing dynamic adapters for heterogeneous wearable clients. Congratulations to first author Hsing-Tzu Ko and sincere thanks to Samuel Yen-Chi Chen for another rewarding collaboration at the intersection of federated learning, wearable intelligence, and quantum-inspired AI. 📍 2026 35th International Conference on Computer Communications and Networks (ICCCN), Honolulu, Hawaii, USA #FederatedLearning #QuantumInspiredAI #WearableIoT #EdgeIntelligence #PersonalizedAI #DigitalHealth #IEEE #ICCCN2026 #PecuLab

    "Come back when you've done your first project." The longer I work with large Japanese clients, the more I recognize many have been developing a "traumatic disorder". Meeting at the headquarters of a Nikkei 225 company in Tokyo, one that serves millions of clients across Japan. Their reaction was in some ways predictable: - What are your references in Japan? - How's that trial project doing? - Who else are you talking to? The product can be state-of-the-art, the business case might make sense. But as long as you lack that first reference, locally, in Japan, it's hard. So, no business with this client just yet. I know I've been preaching it for years. References and social proof are key to convincing Japanese customers. Avoiding uncertainty is at the core of their DNA. But when it happens again, it can be frustrating. The thing is: You need a Japanese reference to win a Japanese client, and vice versa. Twenty five years in, I still don't have a good answer. The projects my overseas clients have won usually came down to a person rather than a proposal. Someone inside who decided that doing nothing was the bigger risk. So how do you overcome that "fear of moving first"?
