<p>Sometimes leaving does not mean you have stopped loving the place you came from.</p>

<p>At a certain stage, you begin to understand that the path entrusted to you may extend beyond the familiar campus, city, or circle of relationships.</p>

<p>This year marks exactly ten years since I returned to Taiwan from Germany.</p>

<p>Over that decade, I have taught, researched, supported interdisciplinary collaboration, and accompanied successive groups of students as they developed abilities, direction, and courage before knowing how far they could go.</p>

<p>Some continued studying, working, and developing their lives in the United States, Canada, Germany, the United Kingdom, Japan, and elsewhere. Others may not have crossed a national border, but crossed a threshold in their own lives they had once thought impossible.</p>

<p>I have always believed that education is not about keeping people beside you.</p>

<p>It is about helping them develop the abilities to take themselves into a wider world one day.</p>

<p>I treasure the years spent accompanying students: helping them see possibilities they once could not believe in and gradually turn uncertain hopes into abilities they could carry with them.</p>

<p>Now, I think it is time to send myself out again too.</p>

<p>After completing my current resignation and handover arrangements, our family of three will make Seattle our primary base for life and work.</p>

<p>This is more than saying, “I am leaving.”</p>

<p>After accompanying so many others forward, I need to practice again: entering unfamiliar territory, acknowledging uncertainty, building new connections from scratch, and trusting that what I have accumulated will not be wasted.</p>

<p>I will remain deeply connected with Taiwan, home to family, students, partners, and stories I cherish. Seattle will become a new base for my work in AI applications, education, research, and international collaboration.</p>

<p>What I want to do remains the same: help good ideas become projects, systems, and actions rather than remain in our heads, and create connections among more people.</p>

<p>I would also like to reconnect with friends and former students living, working, or building businesses across the United States. Whatever you are doing, and whatever kind of new beginning you are experiencing, you are welcome to reach out.</p>

<p>Perhaps some paths do not have to be walked alone.</p>

<p>Perhaps new possibilities begin with a sincere encounter.</p>
