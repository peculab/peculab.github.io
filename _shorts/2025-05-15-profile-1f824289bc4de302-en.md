<p>The midterm review of the Educational Big Data microprogram happened to include my Educational Big Data Project Development course. The reviewers came into the classroom to observe, and the actual proceedings are publicly available on YouTube: https://youtu.be/Be22O6Y305I?si=2XfDPkxPPlH6xMPk</p>

<p>I did not prepare a special presentation or scripted answers. We simply talked as we normally would about how the class works, how students develop ideas, and how I observe and guide them.</p>

<p>To give the reviewers a more direct view of what students had invested in this semester, I brought forward the project presentations originally planned for the end of term. Students showed how they had built their own work from the ground up.</p>

<p>The course's central principle is to let students begin with their own curiosity and decide which problems to solve, technologies to try, and experiences to accumulate—to learn for themselves.</p>

<p>Three teams presented that day. Each demonstrated independent thinking, a willingness to experiment, and solid hands-on work. I felt enormously proud and moved.</p>

<p>Team one: educational platform data analysis and a personalized learning system.</p>

<p>In this interdisciplinary team, 楊嘉翔 from Business Administration focused on data analysis, while 郭至浩 from Physics developed the system. 嘉翔 analyzed real data from the 力宇教育 digital learning platform, examining motivation, learning styles, and participation to build a nuanced picture of learners. Clustering models helped the team investigate individual students' weak points.</p>

<p>至浩's system automatically recommended personalized exercises. It was more than a polished technical demonstration: it addressed real teaching needs and challenges, considering how data could help students overcome learning barriers and build the confidence and ability to learn from mistakes.</p>

<p>Team two: a Gmail phishing email detection and analysis tool.</p>

<p>This team, 邱鈺婷 from Technology Application and Human Resource Development and 涂允貞 from Electrical Engineering, chose a pressing everyday problem: identifying phishing and scam messages in Gmail quickly and effectively.</p>

<p>They built a browser extension using an autoencoder for anomalous URL analysis. Despite limited data and less-than-ideal model conditions, they kept seeking a workable approach. The result supported both batch scans and individual-message analysis, marking risks directly in the interface to help users recognize potential threats.</p>

<p>What I appreciated most was their practical attitude. Rather than imagining solutions under ideal conditions, they built within real constraints, embodying something I often tell students: “Technology is not the ultimate goal; it is a tool for putting ideas into practice and improving life.”</p>

<p>Team three: a multifunctional Discord AI assistant bot.</p>

<p>Two students from Technology Application and Human Resource Development and one from Electrical Engineering built a modular assistant combining translation, YouTube playback, scheduling, cybersecurity attack simulation, and article summarization. Their integration of AI technologies and APIs showed strong engineering and user-centered thinking.</p>

<p>I was particularly impressed that they kept asking how people would actually use the features. Their attention to workflows and interaction made the functions meaningful and engaging, rather than simply numerous.</p>

<p>Through synchronous and asynchronous collaboration on these projects, the three teams experienced the challenges and satisfaction of building a system from nothing. They also came to understand the importance of teamwork and integration.</p>

<p>Looking back over the semester, students did not necessarily start with strong programming or analytical skills. They encountered setbacks along the way. But if they were willing to begin with a real problem, take the first step with curiosity and courage, and keep working, the course had already fulfilled its purpose.</p>

<p>I often tell students that I am not teaching a standard answer. I am here to help them break problems down, get past obstacles, and work through uncertainty and difficulty.</p>

<p>I am happy to have shared this journey with them. I believe they will carry that spirit of practice forward and influence others. Thank you to my students for everything you have taught me and for the experiences that have moved me.</p>
