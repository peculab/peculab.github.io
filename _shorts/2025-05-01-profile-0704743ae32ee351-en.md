<p>Python AI Hands-on Introduction, second semester of academic year 113 (2024–2025): from everyday issues to practical applications.</p>

<p>I strongly recommend watching from 17:38: https://www.youtube.com/live/A5G9iFJ6gMU?si=7d46mUFbwM2AYn1s</p>

<p>In today's class, I could clearly feel the influence of our teaching approach beginning to show.</p>

<p>陳秉銨, a student from Kaohsiung Senior High School, has made a remarkable breakthrough after attending the live online course for less than two months.</p>

<p>He has not merely learned to run text-mining code. He can apply it in real situations and develop his own logic for processing data. This is precisely what I have long sought in my teaching design.</p>

<p>My approach emphasizes more than showing students how a piece of code runs. It guides them toward the essence of programming:</p>

<p>turning their understanding, classifications, and rules about data into executable logic.</p>

<p>I use the following steps to help students build this thinking gradually:</p>

<p>1. Examples and imitation: learn basic operations, break tasks down, and understand the data-processing workflow.</p>

<p>2. Independent experimentation and mistakes: bring in data beyond the classroom examples, encounter new challenges, and begin developing personal classification and analysis logic.</p>

<p>3. AI assistance and refinement: use an LLM to produce a first draft of code, adjust it, and learn to make AI an aid to problem-solving.</p>

<p>4. Verification and reflection: compare results with expectations, trace back and revise both code and reasoning, and develop the ability to correct oneself.</p>

<p>This process is an excellent example of what I mean when I say teaching has changed.</p>

<p>When students move beyond basic operation and use programming to express their own reasoning and solve real problems, they advance from receiving knowledge to applying and creating it.</p>

<p>It is particularly important that one student's breakthrough can strongly encourage others.</p>

<p>Among the roughly one hundred students watching today's livestream, a concrete example like this is a direct and effective stimulus. When peers realize, “So that is possible,” this way of thinking can spread through the learning community and deepen its learning and teaching.</p>

<p>That is what I most hope to achieve through this course:</p>

<p>students not only learn, but begin thinking and solving problems in their own ways, influencing and encouraging others to progress too.</p>

<p>In fact, my teaching approach and style began practicing what I describe here as Vibe Coding ten years ago: problem-oriented programming education combining exploration and practical work.</p>

<p>Back then, students mainly worked back and forth between me and Google Search as they explored and learned.</p>

<p>Today, with LLM assistance, that learning process has undergone a new and substantial shift.</p>

<p>This is a post celebrating my own work. I believe I have been working at the leading edge of this change.</p>

<p>The educational principles I have held for years are finally becoming mainstream and bearing fruit.</p>
