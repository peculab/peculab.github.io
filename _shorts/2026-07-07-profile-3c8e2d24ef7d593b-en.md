<p>On the day the United States marked its 250th anniversary, I began writing with a very ordinary question:</p>

<p>How do children gradually grow?</p>

<p>Not only in classrooms, and not simply through a tightly packed schedule.</p>

<p>Their understanding of the world and their own direction emerge gradually through family, school, libraries, community activities, repeated questions, and moments when they are willing to try again.</p>

<p>This is the first piece in my Seattle K-12 Field Notes.</p>

<p>I will try to record everyday moments in Seattle communities without disrupting my child's life or disclosing private details about Shushu, writing from the perspective of a parent and educator who is still learning.</p>

<p>These observations do not represent the whole United States, nor are they intended to rank places. I simply want to notice how learning gradually happens when families, schools, libraries, and communities leave children some room.</p>

<p>As AI becomes increasingly capable, perhaps this is something worth considering together:</p>

<p>Beyond adding more classes, can we also leave children time to explore, ask questions, get to know the world, and gradually get to know themselves?</p>
