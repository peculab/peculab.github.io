<p>Not everyone shares the same starting line, but everyone deserves a sense of direction.</p>

<p>Over more than eight years, I have accompanied students through the choices, exploration, and struggles of continuing their education.</p>

<p>Some come from middle-income or ordinary households. Their families cannot arrange renowned teachers, camps, and competitions, and do not know how to prepare learning portfolios.</p>

<p>These children are not unwilling to work. No one has shown them how to find a direction worth working toward.</p>

<p>They have to build a path themselves, bit by bit.</p>

<p>From my own upbringing, I know how difficult simply finding steady ground can be for families with limited resources.</p>

<p>But I also believe education should take more than one form.</p>

<p>Today's admissions system describes itself as offering multiple routes through examinations, applications, and selection. Yet we see more anxiety, not less. I say this to describe a social phenomenon, rather than to criticize the system.</p>

<p>Students prepare for entrance examinations while rushing to compile portfolios. They worry both that their scores will not secure a place and that their application materials will not look impressive enough.</p>

<p>Many do not really know why they are doing it all. They only know that “everyone does it, and not doing it feels unsafe.”</p>

<p>Alongside those working hard are children born into a different script.</p>

<p>They have options without examinations, family connections, tuition funding, fallback plans, and ways out.</p>

<p>Let me say this honestly: education has never been truly equal.</p>

<p>This is acceptance, not complaint. I hope that I and the people I can reach can recognize the circumstances and make the choices best suited to ourselves.</p>

<p>Some people are born into first class.</p>

<p>Many others have never had a direct route. They find their way one stop and one transfer at a time.</p>

<p>I like to compare education with travel.</p>

<p>Some choose a group tour, some prefer independent travel, and some seek adventure.</p>

<p>Each approach has advantages, risks, and costs that must be accepted.</p>

<p>You cannot choose an adventure trip while expecting the private transfers of a group tour.</p>

<p>You cannot pursue examination and application routes simultaneously and expect all the benefits of both without tradeoffs.</p>

<p>What we need is not for every path to be effortless.</p>

<p>We need to know clearly which path we are taking and what we are willing to give for it.</p>

<p>Education should be navigation, not elimination.</p>

<p>It is not about making everyone start at the same line, but helping each person find a sense of direction.</p>

<p>As a teacher, I cannot change every system. I can accompany children who are willing to work, help them see the road and find their footing, and keep them from getting lost in anxiety and confusion.</p>

<p>May we become a light along a child's path rather than a source of pressure in their heart.</p>

<p>Clarity matters more than effort alone.</p>

<p>Knowing why we are working is what makes it possible to travel far and steadily.</p>
