<p>Over the past two years, AI development has entered a period of intense competition. Many technical teams are racing toward larger models, faster inference, and more refined fine-tuning.</p>

<p>At Jubo, however, we have chosen a different path. We do not believe AI's value comes from expanding parameter counts. It comes from becoming infrastructure that everyone can use seamlessly without understanding its inner workings.</p>

<p>AI should be like electricity: eventually a foundation for daily life and industry, rather than an arena accessible only to a few technical specialists.</p>

<p>Fine-tuning has been an important path in AI's progress, but our practical work has revealed problems that cannot simply be ignored.</p>

<p>When AI relies on fine-tuning for different applications, changing business requirements mean adjusting the model again. This adds time and resource costs and makes large-scale deployment difficult. Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, can give AI access to current information, but retrieval can also introduce incorrect information. In some settings, that risk is unacceptable. In nursing-record queries and decisions, for example, incorrect information could influence clinicians' judgment and even endanger patients.</p>

<p>We therefore need an AI mechanism that can maintain accuracy while adapting to change, rather than relying solely on fine-tuning or RAG. A fine-tuned model also remains a form of static learning: after deployment, its knowledge is bounded by its training data. When business requirements change, difficulty adapting promptly can become a barrier to adoption rather than a benefit.</p>

<p>We recognized that AI's real value is not merely in teaching a model more content, but in enabling timely retrieval and adaptation to provide reliable, dynamically updated information.</p>

<p>We therefore chose an Information Retrieval (IR) + LLM + Data Governance approach. AI can extract key information from current data instead of relying only on past training, while rigorous data governance supports accuracy and consistency, providing the reliability and flexibility enterprise applications require.</p>

<p>Semantic retrieval from current data makes AI more flexible and can help companies integrate it into workflows more quickly, rather than depend on closed, static learning.</p>

<p>Our Jubo N-Copilot was developed around this idea. It is more than an AI assistant: it is an intelligent interface integrating data and supporting decisions.</p>

<p>In the past, enterprise software required people to learn different tools and adapt to different interfaces. Now we make AI the UI, enabling users to communicate with important internal data through natural language.</p>

<p>This lowers the difficulty of adopting AI and helps it become operational infrastructure rather than simply a technical demonstration.</p>

<p>The idea aligns with a recent view expressed by Microsoft's Satya Nadella. In a talk, he described LLMs as more than tools: they could replace SaaS as a new generation of intelligent communication platforms.</p>

<p>Our practical work demonstrates the feasibility of this concept. Jubo N-Copilot is not just one application, but an intelligent layer integrating enterprise data, simplifying management workflows, and improving decision efficiency.</p>

<p>This changes the conventional SaaS experience. Instead of switching among applications, users can obtain information and make decisions through AI directly.</p>

<p>That is the direction we have pursued at Jubo: not only improving enterprise efficiency, but making the relationship between people and data more intuitive and natural.</p>

<p>We therefore take a different view of the rise of new AI models such as DeepSeek. Many worry that new models will increase competitive pressure or disrupt the existing ecosystem.</p>

<p>At Jubo, we do not see that as a problem. We welcome the development because AI's future will not simply be a zero-sum contest over whose model is stronger. It will be a new contest over who makes AI more usable.</p>

<p>Consider electricity. Society does not depend on a single generation method. Hydropower, thermal power, solar, and other technologies combine into a stable supply system.</p>

<p>AI will similarly develop into infrastructure rather than remain a contest centered on a single technology.</p>

<p>When AI becomes infrastructure, the meaningful competition will concern flexible, intuitive applications rather than model strength alone.</p>

<p>Our goal has never been the “strongest AI,” but the AI people can most readily use. N-Copilot lets managers query key data in natural language without learning analytical tools, and lets nursing staff retrieve patient information without adapting to complex systems. This is central to making AI an everyday tool.</p>

<p>In the future, AI will no longer be reserved for technical elites. Like electricity, it will extend throughout society so everyone can use it seamlessly.</p>

<p>Real change will happen where AI makes work and life easier, not simply where teams compete over parameter counts. We do not need everyone to become an AI engineer, just as we do not need everyone to understand electricity generation.</p>

<p>But we can work to ensure that people can use AI to improve their capabilities, make decisions more efficiently, give data more value, and put technology to human use.</p>

<p>That is the future Jubo sees beyond the AI arms race, and the direction we have been working toward.</p>
