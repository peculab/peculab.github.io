<p>At a Ministry of Education press conference last September, Teacher Su asked, “Children love playing video games. What should we do?” The room fell quiet as everyone seemed to consider this apparently difficult question.</p>

<p>But Teacher Su answered without hesitation: “Don't stop them from playing games. Guide them to use AI to design game battles!” His words immediately caught everyone's attention.</p>

<p>To make the idea concrete, he demonstrated a platform he had designed: a Scratch-based tool that lets children use simple graphical operations to create AI-driven game characters and even have those characters compete. Games became a place to develop creativity and logical thinking, rather than simply a pastime.</p>

<p>The idea did not stay on the stage. At the end of last November, PTWA decided to bring this technology and educational approach to an AI game-design winter camp for children in Taitung during this year's winter break. Local children were excited as soon as they heard and eager to create games of their own.</p>

<p>On the camp's first day, children began exploring AI's basic logic from scratch. Initially, the unfamiliar technology made them a little nervous. But with every module dragged onto the screen and every character action successfully designed, their confidence and enthusiasm grew. Some shared ideas and challenged themselves to make their characters stronger in battle.</p>

<p>They experienced the deep satisfaction of not merely playing games, but making games themselves.</p>

<p>By the end of the two-day camp, the children had completed their own AI games and challenged one another and shared their work at the closing session.</p>

<p>Seeing their growth and engagement moved PTWA members and Teacher Xiaoying. This was more than a lesson in technology; it opened a view of the future.</p>

<p>Through activities like this, we show children that technology is not beyond their reach. Everyone can take part and create.</p>

<p>The winter camp sparked Taitung children's enthusiasm for game design and showed another educational possibility: when we move from resisting games to guiding children in using technology, they can become creators of the future rather than only consumers.</p>

<p>That is education's real power: opening possibilities for every child through creativity.</p>

<p>Teacher Su's answer traveled from a press conference into the hands-on experience of an enthusiastic group of children at a winter camp in Taitung.</p>
