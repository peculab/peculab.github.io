<p>If you are considering bringing your child to the United States this summer to experience a different learning environment, I would like to share this opportunity with you.</p>

<p>From July 27–31 this year, Junior NATEA will hold its first Robotics, CS &amp; AI Summer Program at the University of Washington Seattle campus. This five-day technology camp is for incoming fifth- through ninth-graders and introduces robotics, Python, computer science, AI, and engineering design thinking through hands-on projects.</p>

<p>For children from Taiwan, participating in an activity like this in Seattle means more than taking a week of classes.</p>

<p>They can learn on the UW campus, collaborate with children from different backgrounds, and see how technology education in the United States encourages children to build, ask, experiment, and solve problems.</p>

<p>Many parents ask me, “Can my child participate if their English is not yet fluent?”<br>
For this kind of camp, English is not the only consideration. What also matters is whether children are willing to encounter new things, try hands-on work, and complete tasks as a team.</p>

<p>This is Junior NATEA's first technology summer camp at UW. Beyond the course itself, many Taiwanese American students are helping organize, lead, and serve, hoping younger children can see themselves becoming organizers, mentors, and leaders in the future.</p>

<p>📍 Location: University of Washington Seattle Campus<br>
📅 Dates: July 27–31, 2026<br>
👦👧 Participants: incoming fifth- through ninth-graders, approximately ages 10–14<br>
🤖 Topics: robotics, Python, CS, AI, and engineering design thinking<br>
⏰ Registration deadline: July 1<br>
🎟️ The last 6 places remain at the time of this post.</p>

<p>Event website:<br>
https://www.juniornateaevents.org/</p>

<p>If you are planning a summer experience in the United States for your child, or want to discuss Seattle transportation, accommodation, or parental accompaniment, you are welcome to message me. I am happy to share what I know to help you assess whether the activity is suitable.</p>

<p>Please also share this with Taiwanese families who may be planning a summer experience in the United States.</p>
